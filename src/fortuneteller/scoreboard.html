FortuneTeller

What followed past events, counted. Pick a kind of event, a jobs report above forecasts or an OPEC+ cut, and see what markets did after every past one. An entertainment tool, not financial advice. The numbers are the ones the code prints.

The answers that hold

Most kinds of event are followed by nothing a count can tell from chance. These are the ones that are, and that also held when every past event was answered only from the ones before it. A move on the day is counted from the close before the news, so much of it can come before you read the headline.

    Ask the fortune teller

    Entertainment, not financial advice. Pick a kind of event and see what each market did after every past one, on the day and over the spans after it, beside what it did over every span of its history. An answer is given only when the counts are unlikely by chance; most read can't say. Each answer was also replayed, every event answered from the ones before it; only a replay that held is marked. A move on the day is counted from the close before the news, so much of it can come before you read the headline.

      The week ahead

      Entertainment, not financial advice. Nothing is called until the number lands; this is the rule it will follow. For each release in the next seven days: what forecasters expect, and what the fortune teller will say if the number lands beyond either threshold. A smaller surprise gets no call. Each market's record is its replay, every release predicted from the ones before it.

      S&P 500 companies reporting

      Entertainment, not financial advice. Nothing is said until the report lands. What a company's shares did against the S&P 500 on the day after past S&P 500 reports of each kind, a direction only where it held; then each company reporting in the next seven weekdays.

        Also on the calendar

        The calls

        Each release's lines as logged that morning: the direction called, the link's record before it as counts, the band half its moves land in, and the grade on the close.

        Beat the fortune teller

        How to play: on GitHub, add a file data/guesses/<day>-<handle>.csv, with the header flow,instrument,direction and one row per market (cpi,SPY / ES,up), and open a pull request that adds only that file. A guess counts only if the owner merges the pull request before 08:30 New York on its day; one merged later is void, and one never merged is not shown. The rules.

        Not a fair fight: a person guesses before the release; the machine calls after it, knowing the actual, and only on the links and days it chooses.

        Public and permanent: a guess stays in the repository's history and on this page. Handles are not checked, so one person can play under two; every handle's full record is shown, losses included. A handle is dropped from the page on request; the history stays.
